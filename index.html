On the floor

What we practice

We train a complete striking system: long range, mid-range, and close range, including clinch work. Contact stays light so you can come back next week and still use your hands at work.

Kata is there to sharpen form, gain new ideas, and for conditioning and coordination. I try to use Japanese terms in class and say the English next to them. I don’t always have the Japanese on the spot.

Our karate is this: meet the attack hard when it should be stopped, and soft when it should be guided past you. Both are the art.

Learn the principle. Mix it. Then make it yours on the floor.

Defense — uke no go gensoku

受けの五原則

Mabuni’s five ways to receive. Principles, not five named blocks. Mix them. Two of the five are below.

Rakka — falling petals

落花

Smash the attack on contact. Hard, twisting. Stops their weapon.

Examples: gedan-barai, age-uke, yoko-uke.

Ryusui — flowing water

流水

Do not crash. Guide their force past you. Soft, circular, continuous.

Examples: nagashi-uke, osae-uke, sukui-uke.

Coming in

The first class

Fees
There are no fees right now. If that changes, we will talk about it in person — not on this page.
What to wear
A t-shirt, sweats or gi pants, and clean feet. You do not need to buy a gi until you know you want to stay.
What we do
Warm-up, stance and striking basics, one receiving principle, short partner work, and light contact if you are ready.
Place and time
South Kitsap, in a church or community space. I will confirm the floor when we pick a time. Mornings and evenings.

I do not post a building until I have it lined up. When a night is regular, it will be there.
